# Holobox Experience Context

## Project Vision

The Holobox is not a touchscreen application.

It is a premium interactive installation built around a transparent holographic display.

Visitors should feel they are approaching a museum exhibit rather than using software.

The software should disappear.

The experience should feel magical, calm, elegant and spatial.

---

# Hardware

The display is a vertical transparent Holobox.

White illuminated interior.

Transparent front glass.

Portrait orientation (9:16).

A real physical object may exist inside the cabinet.

The software must always respect the physical installation.

---

# Physical Centerpiece

For the Golf Tournament experience, the centerpiece is a real trophy.

The trophy is the visual hero.

Everything else exists to frame and celebrate it.

The software should never compete with the trophy.

---

# Physical Safe Zone

The center of the display is reserved for the physical object.

No UI elements should permanently occupy this space.

Digital elements may temporarily pass in front of or behind the trophy when appropriate, but the trophy should remain readable.

The physical measurements provided in the installation reference are the source of truth.

Respect the measured safe area and margins.

---

# Digital Centerpiece

The engine also supports a Digital CenterPiece mode.

When enabled:

- the physical pedestal disappears
- the GLB trophy is rendered digitally
- the interaction remains identical

CTRL + D toggles between Physical and Digital modes.

---

# Experience Flow

Idle

↓

Photos orbit around the trophy.

↓

User touches a photo.

↓

The selected photo leaves the orbit.

↓

The photo moves toward the visitor.

↓

Remaining photos slow down.

↓

The trophy remains visible.

↓

User may:

- browse next / previous
- open full gallery
- close and return

↓

The selected photo returns naturally to the orbit.

↓

The installation returns to idle.

---

# Photos

The experience is not limited to player portraits.

Photos represent memories.

Tournament moments.

Behind-the-scenes.

Celebrations.

People.

History.

Video memories.

The content tells the story around the trophy.

---

# Orbit

The orbit should never feel mechanical.

It should feel alive.

Requirements:

- irregular movement
- subtle breathing
- depth
- slight variation
- photos continuously cycle through the visible orbit
- smooth motion
- premium easing

Not a carousel.

Not a perfect ellipse.

Think of objects suspended in air.

---

# Motion

Motion is one of the product's defining features.

Every transition should feel intentional.

No abrupt movement.

Motion should have:

- anticipation
- overlap
- easing
- follow-through
- settling

The installation should feel expensive.

---

# Visual Language

Minimal.

Museum quality.

Apple Vision Pro.

Rolex.

Leica.

Luxury exhibitions.

Invisible interface.

Glass.

Large spacing.

Elegant typography.

Very little text.

The content is always more important than the interface.

---

# Decorations

Top and bottom decorations frame the experience.

They are configurable per project.

They should never dominate the scene.

Think exhibition graphics rather than UI chrome.

---

# Particles

Particles should feel environmental.

Examples:

- dust
- pollen
- floating light

Never:

- sparks
- explosions
- gaming particles

Particles should reinforce depth.

---

# Gallery

The gallery is an extension of the orbit.

It should emerge naturally from the current scene.

Not replace it.

Visitors should always feel they are inside the same installation.

---

# Performance Goals

The Holobox runs on a PC inside the cabinet.

Target:

- 60 FPS
- Offline-first
- Smooth interaction
- Fast startup
- Stable playback

Visual quality should never compromise responsiveness.

---

# Design Philosophy

People should not remember the interface.

People should remember the trophy.

People should remember the memories.

People should remember the feeling.

The best compliment is:

"I don't know how they made that."
